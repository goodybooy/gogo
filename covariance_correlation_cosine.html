Covariance vs Correlation vs Cosine

Which similarity measure to reach for, and why they are not interchangeable

? The decision rule

Ask two questions, in order:

  1. Do I need the answer in units (dollars, vol, risk)? → COVARIANCE
    It is the only one that preserves magnitude, and it is what enters every risk formula.
  2. Are the vector entries repeated observations of the same quantity, or different features of one object?
    • Observations over time → CORRELATION  (centering is meaningful)
    • Features of an object → COSINE  (no meaningful mean along that axis)

1 Covariance — when magnitude matters

Use caseWhy
Portfolio variance w'Σw Correlation alone cannot give you portfolio vol.
Risk decomposition (MCR / TCR) (Σx)ᵢ is in return units — needed for risk budgeting.
Optimal hedge ratio β = Cov(y,x)/Var(x) This is a quantity of the hedge instrument, not a similarity.
Kalman filters, GLS, Mahalanobis distance The math requires the actual covariance operator.
Optimizer inputs (mean–variance, risk parity) Σ is the native input.
Rule: if the output is a position size or a risk number, you need covariance.

2 Correlation — when comparing across different-scaled series

Use caseWhy
Strategy diversification screening Comparing a 5%-vol carry book to a 20%-vol trend book — units must be removed.
Factor correlation / redundancy checks Is my new signal already spanned? VIF and orthogonalization live here.
PCA on returns Correlation-PCA when scales differ wildly; covariance-PCA when they are comparable.
Regime analysis “Did equity–bond correlation flip in 2022?” — a unitless, comparable-over-time quantity.
Pairs / RV candidate screening First-pass filter before running cointegration.
Reporting to a PM or risk committee “0.7 correlated” is interpretable; “covariance of 0.00013” is not.
Rule: default for anything involving time series of returns. This is 90% of your work.

3 Cosine — when entries are features, not observations

Use caseWhy
Book similarity / crowding — cos(w_A, w_B) on position vectors Entries are instruments (features); scale invariance is desirable — half the gross is the same strategy.
PM overlap in a multi-strat Are two pods running the same trade?
Sector / country exposure similarity Comparing allocation shapes.
Text / NLP work (earnings calls, Fed statements, news) Sparse non-negative counts; centering destroys sparsity.
Embedding retrieval Models are trained with cosine objectives.
Rule: the vector describes one object across many attributes, not one variable across many time points.

4 The quick disambiguator

Same two strategies, three different questions:

Cov(r_A, r_B)   →  "how much risk do they add to each other in my book?"     [dollars]
Corr(r_A, r_B)  →  "do their P&Ls move together?"                            [unitless]
cos(w_A, w_B)   →  "are they holding the same positions?"                     [structure]
These genuinely differ. Two books can hold near-identical positions (cos = 0.95) but have uncorrelated returns (r = 0.1) if one is trading them at different tenors or with different timing. Conversely two books with zero position overlap (cos = 0) can be highly correlated (r = 0.8) through a common macro driver. Crowding risk and correlation risk are different risks, and you need both measures to see them.

! Common mistakes

  • Cosine on uncentered returns — the sample mean inflates it toward 1; distortion scales with the Sharpe of your window (fine daily, bad annually).
  • Mixing cosine and correlation in one matrix — different null values; downstream clustering / PCA becomes garbage.
  • Correlation into an optimizer without re-scaling by vols — silently equal-weights risk.
  • Covariance-PCA on mixed-scale data — the highest-vol asset dominates PC1 mechanically; use correlation-PCA unless the scales are genuinely comparable.
  • Reading correlation as dependence — r = 0 is linear independence only; tail dependence survives it, which is precisely what kills you in crises.
