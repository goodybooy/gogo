# Owner (owner.com) — Quick Research Brief

*Compiled 2026-09-20. ~3 minute read.*

## What it is

Owner is an **AI-native growth platform for independent restaurants**. It builds and runs the
restaurant's *demand side*: website, direct online ordering, branded iOS/Android app, CRM,
email/SMS/push marketing, loyalty, SEO automation, delivery coordination, AI phone ordering —
and, more recently, POS.

Positioning in their own words: the **"AI CMO and CTO for local businesses."** Not a cash
register — a customer-acquisition engine.

**Pricing:** month-to-month, no contracts. Flex **$249/mo + 5%** per order, or Flat-Rate
**$499/mo + 0% commission**. Customer pays a 5% order fee; delivery ~$7/order via a vetted
driver network. No hardware required. Launch in 2–7 days.

## Why it can compete with Toast and Square

### 1. It attacks a different problem — and a bigger line item

Toast and Square sell **operational efficiency**: order-taking, payments, labor, hardware.
Owner sells **revenue growth**. The pain it targets isn't the register, it's the
**25–40% commission** DoorDash/Uber Eats/Grubhub extract from an industry running 3–5% margins.
Third-party marketplaces control ~80% of delivery orders. That's the wound, and it's far more
expensive to a restaurant than POS software.

### 2. The ROI is arithmetic, not a pitch

A restaurant doing $20K/month in delivery moves from ~25% commission to a $499 flat fee and
saves roughly **$5,000/month**. The subscription pays for itself several times over in month
one. Owner also claims +30% direct online revenue in year one, guests 2x more likely to order
on an Owner site, and +30% traffic within 30 days. A POS vendor can't make a comparable
top-line promise — it can only offer to cost less.

### 3. It lands *beside* the incumbents instead of fighting them

This is the sharpest part of the strategy. Owner **integrates with Toast, Square, and Clover
rather than replacing them** — "hundreds of Owner's customers use these systems in addition to
Owner." So there's no rip-and-replace, no hardware swap, no retraining, no multi-year contract
to break. Switching cost approaches zero, which neutralizes the incumbents' single biggest
defense: POS lock-in. Owner gets in, proves ROI on the demand side, and only then expands into
POS and payments from a position of trust.

### 4. Flat fee vs. take rate is a wedge, not just a price

Toast and Square monetize a **percentage of every transaction**. Owner charges a **flat
subscription**. As a restaurant's direct volume grows, the incumbent's bill scales with it while
Owner's doesn't — so Owner's pitch gets stronger exactly as the customer succeeds. It also
aligns the vendor with growth rather than throughput, and expansion revenue arrives through
order volume and new locations instead of price increases.

### 5. AI replaces labor the SMB could never afford

An independent operator cannot hire a CMO, a web developer, and an agency. Owner's "AI
Executives" (CMO/CTO/CFO agents) target that gap — the alternative isn't competing software,
it's **work that simply never gets done**. Owner's data across thousands of restaurants and
tens of millions of consumers feeds the personalization, which compounds.

### 6. Distribution is unusually efficient

Content-led inbound — Owner is deliberately building itself into the authority on restaurant
growth, so operators arrive pre-educated. Result: **90%+ of account executives ramp to quota
within 3 months**. For an SMB motion where CAC usually kills the model, that's the hard part
solved.

### 7. The vertical wedge generalizes

Restaurants are the beachhead, not the market. Owner plans to extend into salons, pet care,
spas, gyms, florists, auto shops, indie grocers — 15+ local-business types with the same
structure: online-dependent demand, no in-house marketing, margin squeezed by aggregators.

## Risks

- **DoorDash dependency.** The zero-commission delivery offer rides on DoorDash's driver
  network. If that pricing changes, a core part of the value proposition is exposed.
- **$499/mo is real money** at 3–5% margins. Works for restaurants with meaningful delivery
  volume (>$5K/mo); harder below that, which caps the addressable base.
- **SMB logo churn** is structurally high — restaurants close. Owner offsets it with expansion
  revenue, but it's a permanent headwind.
- **23x revenue** leaves no room for a growth stumble.
- **Incumbent response.** Toast and Square already have POS lock-in and payments economics; they
  can bundle online ordering aggressively and subsidize it.

## Bottom line

Owner isn't beating Toast and Square at their own game — it's **refusing to play it**. It picked
the demand side, where the incumbents are weak and the customer's pain is 10x larger; it made
switching nearly free by integrating rather than replacing; and it priced as a flat fee against
their take rates. Classic wedge: win a problem the incumbent treats as a feature, then expand
into their core. The POS launch is that expansion beginning.
